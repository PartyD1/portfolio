## Design Specificity Verdict

**Grounded, not template-interchangeable.** Three decisions could not survive a find-and-replace onto an unrelated portfolio: the flagship card is titled "Summer 2026 Internship" and *deliberately omits* a repo link rather than disabling one — a generic template defaults to always showing a GitHub icon; the Experience timeline links each role directly to the Work card it produced, which only makes sense under this site's specific thesis that employment and project are proof of each other; and the hero's six-word descriptor roll lands its accent color only on "obsessed with AI," encoding the actual positioning claim rather than a generic tagline. The visual world itself (iridescent blobs, outline-and-fill headline) is a consciously borrowed reference per the homepage direction contract, so the chrome is less distinctive than the content architecture — but the architecture is where the specificity actually lives, and it's real.

**Deterministic scan:** `detect.mjs --json` against `app/page.tsx`, every homepage component, all four `data/*.ts` files, and `app/globals.css` (run separately per the detector's markup-vs-CSS split) returned exit code 0 with zero findings on both invocations. No boilerplate-pattern or generic-copy rule fired.

**Browser evidence, and a corrected finding:** Fresh screenshots were captured against the current build (not the committed `.impeccable/review/` set, which predates a hero amendment and the whole mobile-overhaul series). At 1440px, both themes, fold and full page: no visual defects. At 390px, Assessment B's supplied evidence showed what looked like a large blank void where Experience/About/Contact should render on the full-page mobile capture — investigated directly (see below) and it is **not a real site defect**. It's an artifact of `page.screenshot({ fullPage: true })` interacting with this project's `content-visibility: auto` phone-perf rule (`app/globals.css`, PR 10 of the mobile overhaul): scrolling back to the top before a single full-page capture leaves those sections "not relevant to the user" at the moment Chromium paints the stitched image, even though their DOM layout (height, position) is provably correct at that instant. Skipping the return-to-top before the shot renders everything correctly. The project's own `scripts/mobile-capture.mjs` already sidesteps this exact trap — it captures each scroll segment while it's on-screen and stitches the segment images afterward, rather than one `fullPage` shot from a scrolled-to-top state — so this is a note for ad hoc capture scripts, not a gap in the project's real QA tooling.

## Overall Impression

This is a disciplined, specific piece of design work that earns its restraint — the absence-over-placeholder rule reads as confidence rather than incompleteness, both themes are genuinely first-class, and the accessibility basics (headings, landmarks, labels, contrast) are clean by direct measurement, not just by intention. The one real structural gap is pacing: the hero's calm, single-decision fold hands off directly into seven simultaneous project cards with no sub-grouping, which is the single biggest jump in cognitive load anywhere on the page.

## What's Working

1. **Absence-over-placeholder discipline actually reads as confidence.** The Operations Agent card turns a real constraint (no public repo) into a specific, calm sentence instead of a dead link or a "coming soon" — exactly the effect `PRODUCT.md`'s merge-gate language is chasing.
2. **Experience↔Work cross-linking is a genuinely uncommon IA move.** Most portfolios keep employment and projects in separate silos; here each role links straight to the project it produced, which is a real, load-bearing argument for credibility, not decoration.
3. **Accessibility and cross-theme parity are solid by measurement, not just design intent.** Zero missing alt text, zero unlabeled interactive elements, correct heading order (H1→H2→H3…), `aria-labelledby` on every section, and contrast ≥6.67:1 (light) / ≥13.26:1 (dark) across sampled text — verified live against the running build, not assumed from the token sheet.

## Priority Issues

- **[P1] The Work section presents 7 simultaneous case-study entry points with no visual sub-chunking.** Why it matters: the Cognitive Load Checklist's "minimal choices" rule caps a single decision point at ≤4 visible options before working memory is overloaded; a recruiter scanning the fold-to-Work transition goes from one clean decision (read the hero) to seven parallel "open this" targets in one visual field, with only the flagship's span size to differentiate them. Fix: either accept this as the flagship-first hierarchy already doing enough work (span + first position), or add a quiet visual break between the flagship/first row and the remaining four cards so the grid reads as two decisions instead of one. Suggested command: `$impeccable layout`.

- **[P2] The incoming, unstarted EduSchool row reads as a motivation dip right after two data-rich rows.** Why it matters: Operations Agent and Augmented Design Lab both give a recruiter real detail to evaluate; the EduSchool row (title, company, "Fall 2026," nothing else) is correctly minimal per the absence rule, but its plainness lands directly after momentum the previous two rows built, which costs some of that momentum. Fix: no new content can be invented (this is a data constraint, not a design one), but a quieter visual treatment that signals "agreed, not yet started" as a distinct state (rather than the same row style as the two live roles) could soften the contrast. Suggested command: `$impeccable layout`.

- **[P2] The hero's mobile fold arrow is the one moment this specific design reverts to a generic affordance.** Why it matters: everywhere else on the page, the details are considered and specific (the outline-and-fill headline, the descriptor roll landing on "obsessed with AI" in accent color); the down-chevron cueing "scroll for work" on phones is visually indistinguishable from the same affordance on a thousand other sites. Fix: give it one small distinguishing trait consistent with the system (e.g., the same wave motif under the name, or a shape unique to this palette) rather than a stock chevron. Suggested command: `$impeccable delight`.

- **[P3] Two different "view repo" treatments exist across project cards with no stated rule for which project gets which.** ScorelyAI shows its repo link in-flow; most other cards show it as a corner icon. Why it matters: minor, but it's an inconsistency a returning or careful visitor (Riley the stress-tester) will notice even if it costs nothing functionally. Fix: pick one treatment, or document the rule that decides between them (e.g., "in-flow when a live-link pill is also present"). Suggested command: `$impeccable polish`.

## Minor Observations

- The wave SVG tracking under "PARTH DOSHI" is a specific, memorable signature touch that holds up cleanly in both themes at both captured widths.
- The `card__fact-sep` middot pattern repeats consistently between hero facts and card facts — good small-scale systemic reuse, not something the detector would ever catch.
- The About section's range pills (Autonomous agents, Game systems & physics, etc.) duplicate categorization already implied by the card copy above them — harmless redundancy, not worth fixing on its own.
